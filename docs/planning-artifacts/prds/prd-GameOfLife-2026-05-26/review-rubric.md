# PRD Quality Review — Game of Life Studio

## Overall verdict

This is a strong, build-ready PRD that has closed the referential-integrity and settings-cluster gaps flagged in prior rounds. The six items reworked since the last run all verify as landed in the current FR text and are internally consistent — most notably the usage-as-union definition, which now genuinely closes the delete/dangling-reference gap rather than papering over it. What holds up: precise done-ness on the hard mechanics (three-phase precedence, Dominance eviction, cell-age and usage union accounting), honest scope with a thorough Non-Goals section, and a single load-bearing protagonist that fits the product's shape. What remains at risk is minor: the Settings cluster is now rationale-backed but still reads as somewhat thesis-adjacent, and a known lowercase casing drift persists in the Settings prose. No critical or high findings.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions and the trade-off given up is named, not smoothed. FR-8.4 keeps import as a destructive whole-workspace replace and says so plainly, pairing it with a mandatory warning + export-first mitigation and a Non-Goals pointer to the deferred merge path (lines 505-515, 711) — the reader sees exactly what was traded (coexistence) for what was chosen (one well-guarded replace). FR-5.4 chooses the "aggressive competition" branch — "a **Born claim can evict a surviving incumbent**" (line 360) — over incumbent protection, and it is honest that this leans into the Dominance thesis rather than hedging. The relative-cell-state gating note (line 361) pre-empts the obvious objection ("then how does Blue hold its corner?") and answers it inline with a UJ cross-reference. Open Questions are genuinely resolved with traceability rather than rhetorical: OQ-1 and OQ-2 both carry explicit `Status: Resolved` dispositions (lines 727, 731). Assumptions A-1–A-4 are tagged inline and indexed.

## Substance over theater — adequate

No persona theater: a single protagonist, David (13), drives real decisions — the theme-accessibility default (A-4), the no-tutorial NFR, and the 1024px-minimum device floor all trace to him. NFRs carry product-specific thresholds rather than boilerplate: 60 FPS at the 100×60 baseline with a named degradation order (NFR-1.1, line 588), `<10ms (p95)` localStorage (NFR-1.4), 30%→100% saturation by age 7 (FR-5.7). The Vision is product-specific — "multi-organism ecological competition" — and would not swap cleanly into another PRD. The one soft spot keeping this from strong: the "intentionally rigorous to demonstrate SDD... portfolio showcase" framing (line 14) is self-aware but means some rigor is present for its own sake; the NFR-5.2 design-patterns list is defended item-by-item as load-bearing (line 634), which is the right instinct, but it still reads partly as showcase furniture.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis — competition resolved by Dominance — is real and the core features serve it: FR-5.4 eviction, FR-2.2 Dominance, and Metric 3 (80% of Battles use 3+ custom organisms with visibly different behaviors, line 748) all validate the same bet, and counter-metrics are present on every metric. The MVP scope kind (experience / problem-solving hobby studio) matches the scope logic. The residual drag is the Settings cluster (FR-8.7–8.12): each now carries a one-line rationale (grid-lines readability line 538, cell-pulse delight line 546, auto-save safety net line 566, default-speed convenience line 576), a real improvement over the prior "feature creep" finding, but these remain preference toggles adjacent to — not derived from — the competition thesis. Acceptable for a hobby studio and no longer a coherence hole; it is why the dimension is adequate rather than strong.

## Done-ness clarity — strong

This is the dimension the rework most improved. The usage-as-union accounting is now unambiguous and testable: FR-1.4 (line 134) defines the collapsed saved+live entry as a **union** that never lets a live erase override a saved reference — "unsaved edits that erase the organism from the open grid do **not** drop the still-persisted saved reference, so deletion stays blocked until the Battle is actually saved without it"; FR-1.7 (line 159) states the same merge/dedup-by-Battle-id rule from the visibility side ("no saved-side reference is ever dropped"); and FR-1.4's integrity guarantee (line 137) is a direct consequence — an engineer can write the "unsaved erase does not unblock delete" test straight from the text. The three are mutually consistent. The simulation mechanics are fully pinned: Die-before-Survive is phase-authoritative regardless of sort (FR-5.2, line 342), explicit-vs-implicit death is distinguished with its neighbor-count consequence (line 343), and Born/Survive compete uniformly by Dominance (FR-5.4). FR-2.4's aging toggle now has concrete ACs tied to FR-5.7's numeric transform and to FR-5.6 age-tracking (lines 180-183), closing the earlier bare-FR gap. Adjectival escape hatches are largely absent; where "gracefully" appears (NFR-7.2, NFR-7.3) it is bounded by a stated behavior ("preserving existing data rather than silently dropping it").

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and worked, not inferred. The Non-Goals section (lines 705-721) carries nine capability-level deferrals each with a one-line reason and internal cross-refs (connected mode, non-localStorage storage, pluggable strategies, OR-logic, redo, Web Worker, light theme, build-time CSS, plus consolidated excluded platforms), and the destructive-import and pluggable-strategy deferrals are echoed at their point of use (FR-8.4, FR-5.1). De-scoping is done in the open: merged/moved/removed FRs are tombstoned in place with "see FR-X" pointers (FR-6.2, FR-7.11, FR-7.12, FR-7.14, FR-8.9) rather than silently renumbered, preserving cross-reference integrity. Assumptions are tagged inline and round-trip to the index (A-1–A-4). Open-items density is low and appropriate for a green-light hobby build: four assumptions, two resolved Open Questions, zero dangling `[NOTE FOR PM]`.

## Downstream usability — strong

Feeds UX / architecture / stories cleanly. The Glossary is present and includes the previously-undefined "Lab Mode," pinned as an interchangeable synonym with "Edit Mode" as canonical (lines 799-800) — the prior downstream MEDIUM is closed. Domain nouns (Battle, Organism, Dominance, Cycle, Workspace) are used consistently and defined once. IDs are contiguous and unique with tombstones preserving continuity; cross-references (FR-1.4↔FR-1.7↔FR-3.12, FR-2.4↔FR-5.7, FR-4.2↔FR-8.12) resolve. Sections largely stand alone — the union logic is deliberately restated in both FR-1.4 and FR-1.7 so each is extractable without "see above." UJ-1 has a single named protagonist carrying context inline. The only downstream nit is the Settings-prose casing drift, recorded under Mechanical notes.

## Shape fit — strong

Correctly shaped as a consumer / hobby product with meaningful UX: one named-protagonist User Journey (David) is load-bearing and sourced by downstream UX, and rigor is calibrated up to Launch level per the stakes decision without over-formalizing — there is exactly one UJ, not a manufactured spread. It is not under-formalized (a consumer product with no UJ) nor over-formalized (UJ density for a single-operator tool). NFRs are user-facing where the product is user-facing (performance, accessibility, device floor) and operational where appropriate (test coverage, hosting cost). The portfolio / SDD framing is disclosed up front (line 14) so the extra traceability rigor reads as intentional, not shape-forcing.
